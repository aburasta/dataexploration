## ⚠️ One substantive flag before you publish

The script follows Shirer's 1960 verdict — "beyond a reasonable doubt … it was the Nazis who planned
the arson and carried it out." **That is not the settled academic position.** Fritz Tobias (1962) and
Hans Mommsen argued that van der Lubbe acted alone, and that lone-arsonist thesis has been the
dominant view in German academic historiography for decades (though seriously contested since — by
Bahar & Kugel, and by the Lennings testimony that surfaced in 2019).

A description that flatly asserts "the Nazis did it, proven" on a topic this well-trodden will draw
informed, citable pushback in the comments, and it undercuts the channel's document-grounded
positioning. So the description **states Shirer's verdict as Shirer's verdict**, names the opposing
scholars, and says the question is contested. This costs ~230 characters and buys three things: it is
accurate, it is defensible in the comments, and it captures the "who really started the Reichstag
fire" / "did the Nazis start the Reichstag fire" debate queries, which are among the highest-volume
long-tail searches on this topic. Recommend keeping it. If you'd rather the episode commit harder to
Shirer, that is your call — but the narration should then acknowledge Tobias somewhere, or the
mismatch between a confident script and a hedged description will read oddly.

## Description — full copy-paste block

```
The Reichstag Fire: at a 1942 birthday lunch, Hermann Göring slapped his thigh and said it out loud — "I set it on fire!" A general at the table testified.

By late February 1933, Adolf Hitler had been Chancellor for four weeks and still lacked what he needed: three Nazi ministers in an eleven-man cabinet, an election eight days away, and no proof of the Communist uprising his party kept promising. Then, on the night of 27 February, the Reichstag burned. Here is the detail most retellings skip — Hermann Göring was President of the Reichstag, and his official residence was joined to the building by an underground heating tunnel. This documentary follows that tunnel, and everything the fire was used to justify.

WHAT THIS EPISODE COVERS
• Goebbels' own diary, written that night, admitting he refused to believe the first phone call — while Papen watched the glow from the Herrenklub with President Hindenburg
• Göring at the scene, "quite beside himself with excitement," ordering Gestapo chief Rudolf Diels before a single fact was established: "Every Communist official must be shot, where he is found"
• Marinus van der Lubbe, the Dutch council-communist picked up days earlier after boasting in a bar that he meant to torch the Reichstag — "a godsend," in Shirer's phrase
• Why the evidence never fitted a lone arsonist: the central hall was ablaze two and a half minutes after van der Lubbe entered, and trial experts testified the main fires used gasoline and chemicals at scattered points
• Karl Ernst, the hotel bellhop turned Berlin SA leader, who led storm troopers through Göring's tunnel and slipped back out
• The Nuremberg testimony: Hans Gisevius — "it was Goebbels who first thought of setting the Reichstag on fire" — and Diels' sworn affidavit that "Goering knew exactly how the fire was to be started"
• The Reichstag Fire Decree, signed by Hindenburg the next morning, suspending seven sections of the constitution — never repealed, and the legal foundation of the Third Reich for twelve years

Then what the fire actually bought. Four thousand Communist officials were arrested within hours, along with Social Democrat and liberal leaders. The Prussian government promised documents proving a Communist plot and never published one. And still, on 5 March, Germans did not hand Hitler a majority: the Nazis took 44 percent, the Centre Party's vote rose, the Social Democrats held. At Leipzig, Georgi Dimitroff defended himself and provoked Göring into threatening him from the stand before the world's press — then walked free, acquitted with all three co-defendants. Only van der Lubbe was convicted, and guillotined. The acquittal enraged Hitler enough that in April 1934 treason cases were stripped from Germany's Supreme Court and handed to a tribunal with no appeal: the Volksgerichtshof, the People's Court. And Karl Ernst, who had walked the tunnel, was murdered in the Night of the Long Knives weeks later, alongside three SA men Shirer names as his accomplices. They knew too much.

QUESTIONS THIS EPISODE ANSWERS
• Who really started the Reichstag fire — the Nazis, or van der Lubbe alone?
• Did Hermann Göring confess to burning the Reichstag?
• What was the Reichstag Fire Decree, and why was it never repealed?

WHO THIS IS FOR: viewers who know the Reichstag fire as a textbook line and want the evidence and the tunnel.
```

## Thumbnail directions

House rule: a single strong, ideally colorized, **real archival photograph of the literal subject**,
minimal or no baked-in text.

- **A — The Reichstag burning (RECOMMENDED).** The well-known night shots of the dome and chamber
  fully alight, 27–28 February 1933. It is the most visually arresting image available on any topic
  in this series, it needs no text to be legible at phone size, and it is instantly recognisable
  while still promising detail. Colorize the flames; keep the dome silhouette dark.
- **B — Göring.** A portrait from the Leipzig trial period, ideally one where he looks pleased.
  Pairs directly with the recommended title's confession hook, and puts a face most viewers
  recognise on the thumbnail. Weaker on its own — combine with a small flame element if you crop it.
- **C — The gutted chamber, daylight.** The burnt-out Sitzungssaal photographed after the fire —
  blackened, roofless, silent. Colder and more editorial than A, and it reads as evidence rather
  than spectacle, which suits the "here is what the investigation found" framing. Best B-test
  against A.
